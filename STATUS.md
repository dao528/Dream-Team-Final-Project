# Status memo: Checkpoint 1 (Oct 23)

We picked Seed 3, the disappearing index effect. At Checkpoint 0 it was the cleaner question, but it depended on one input we didn't know we could get: the date S&P announces each addition, as opposed to the date it takes effect. That turned out to be easy. The S&P Dow Jones Indices newsroom can be searched by keyword and paged with plain `requests`, and every "Set to Join S&P 500" release since 2022 has a summary table (effective date, index, action, company, ticker) that `pd.read_html` reads, plus an Eastern-time publication stamp in the page source. That gave us 84 additions from 53 releases, January 2022 to October 2026, with no agent and no hand collection. Seed 7 (analyst rating changes) would have been faster to build, but most of yfinance's rows are reiterations, and ranking 40+ brokers raises a multiple-testing problem we'd rather not build the project around.

The proof of concept (`run_all.py`, steps 01 to 06) builds the full list of additions with both dates, then the event panel for five of them: COIN, DASH, FLEX, MRVL, and VEEV, 210 rows (5 additions x 2 anchors x 21 trading days). The clearest result is COIN: announced May 12, 2025 after the close, it rose 23.97% on May 13, which matches the 24% in the press coverage, and CAR[0,+1] around the announcement is +24.0%. Our hand check (`code/06_hand_check.py`) recomputes that number with `np.polyfit` and positional indexing and gets the same beta and CAR. FLEX's effective-day AR is +6.0%, close to the +6.1% from our first script. Three things broke or surprised us. Wikipedia's changes table (now gone from the live page, so we pin revision 1368675864) lists 83 additions since 2022, but 5 of them are ticker changes or a merger (CPAY, DAY, EG, WTW, WBD), so a Wikipedia-only event study would include 5 fake events; we now treat the S&P releases as the source and Wikipedia as a cross-check, and the two agree on the effective date for 77 of the 78 additions that appear in both (the exception is a market holiday). The release tables use merged cells, so `read_html` repeats headers and values, and we had to pick columns by content. And two of the five events have earnings inside the [-10,+10] window (COIN on May 8, MRVL on May 27), which shows up as noise in the long-window CARs (MRVL: +44%).

Our questions for you:

1. S&P announces after the close, so we set the announcement day 0 to the next trading day. For the effective date, we set day 0 to the first trading day as a member, which means index funds trade at the close of day -1. Is that the convention you want, or should day 0 be the rebalance close?
2. Additions come in batches (51 of 84 share a release with another addition), and some batches announce and take effect within 4 trading days, so the two windows overlap. We plan to cluster standard errors by announcement release and report CAR over short windows such as [0,+1] and [-1,+1]. Is that enough, or should we use a calendar-time portfolio?
3. Several additions are spin-offs with little or no return history before the announcement (Constellation Energy, GE HealthCare, Kenvue, Solventum, GE Vernova, Veralto, Qnity, Solstice, Honeywell Aerospace, FedEx Freight). We plan to drop events with fewer than 200 estimation days and report how many we drop. Does that sound right?
4. For the "share of float held by index funds" cross-section, do you know a free point-in-time source? yfinance only gives today's holders and market cap, which would be look-ahead.
